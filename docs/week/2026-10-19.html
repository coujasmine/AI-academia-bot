Abstract This research investigates the relationship between economic inequality and sustainable consumption, highlighting how socioeconomic disparities shape consumer behavior. Across three studies—using secondary data from 20 countries, a vignette experiment, and UK survey data—we provide empirical evidence that individuals report lower levels of sustainable consumption when inequality levels are objectively high, perceived as high, or experimentally manipulated as high.
